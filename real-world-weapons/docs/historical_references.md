# Historical references and how they were used

This file summarises the evidence behind the three swords and states, part by part, what is
**historical evidence** and what is a **Minecraft adaptation**. The complete research notes are in
[`research/historical_dossier.md`](research/historical_dossier.md). They contain about 190 cited URLs, every
specimen record, and labelled measurements: **[S]** for a published specimen figure, **[T]** for a
typical range and **[I]** for an inference.

> **Caveat from the research pass.** The session's network policy blocked direct page loads from
> metmuseum.org. Museum figures came from search-engine extracts of the official collection
> records and were cross-checked where possible. Spot-check any figure against the live record
> before citing it elsewhere.

## Scale used for all three models

* **1 Bedrock model unit = 5 cm.** The Bedrock player model is 32 units tall and its arm is 12 units
  long. Capoferro (1610) sizes the rapier as "twice as long as the arm". That gives a 24-unit
  (≈122 cm) rapier, which matches the museum specimens.
* Lengths of blades, ricassos, grips and tsuka follow the reference proportions below.
* The documented **adaptations** are listed per weapon. They exist because a Minecraft fist is a
  4 × 4-unit block (≈20 cm at this scale, about twice a real hand) and because pixel-art blades need
  some extra width to stay legible.

### Handling → animation

* **Grip and guards (Fabris 1606, Capoferro 1610):** guard positions are defined by hand rotation.
  Idle is *terza* (hand natural, edges vertical, point toward the opponent), with the left hand held
  before the chest ready to parry, as in Capoferro's plates.
* **Attacks.** The lunge is the main attack. Capoferro equates the sword's length with the lunge step.
  * *Stoccata*: straight lunging thrust; the front leg steps forward and the rear arm is thrown back for balance.
  * *Imbroccata*: descending thrust from a high, pronated hand.
  * *Punta riversa*: thrust from the inside line with the hand supinated.
* **Recovery:** return to terza by withdrawing the front foot. This is general HEMA practice [I]; no
  treatise text on it was retrieved.
* Measured points of balance lie 10–17 cm ahead of the guard [S]. The motions are therefore
  point-led thrusts with small wrist rotations, not sabre cuts.

### Handling → animation

* **Two hands on a long grip** [S, Shore; Melville]. The left hand is placed on the grip with inverse
  kinematics. Measured over every attack frame, it stays within 0.41 units of the grip; a Minecraft
  fist is 4 units.
* **Method.** The Iberian *montante* (Godinho 1599; Figueyredo 1651) is the best surviving written
  method for a true two-hander: continuous cuts against several opponents [S, secondary]. The three
  attacks chain a high diagonal (*Oberhau*), a rising backhand (*Unterhau*) and a horizontal cut
  (*Mittelhau*), each flowing through instead of stopping dead. This drives the gameplay **sweep**.
* **Marching carry** on the shoulder when not fighting is a gameplay pose suggested by period
  depictions [I].
* The "Doppelsöldner broke pike hedges" story is a probable myth [S, secondary] and is **not** used.

### Handling → animation

* Worn **edge-up** through the obi on the left hip [S, Kyoto National Museum]. The equip animation is
  a draw from the left hip into chūdan. This draw also triggers the gameplay **iai draw-cut**,
  modelled on iaijutsu (e.g. Musō Jikiden Eishin-ryū).
* **Grip:** right hand behind the tsuba, left hand at the kashira [S].
* **Guard:** *chūdan-no-kamae*. *Gedan* (low) is used while sneaking, and a raised guard while airborne.
* **Cuts:** *kesa-giri* (down diagonal), *kiri-age* (rising diagonal) and *kiri-oroshi* (vertical) [S].
  The third ends with a *yoko-chiburi* flick to the right, after the Mae sequence of Musō Jikiden
  Eishin-ryū [S].

## The seven material tiers

Only the **Iron** tier reproduces a historical finish. **Wooden** follows real training practice:
*bokken* for the katana and wooden *wasters* for European swords. The others are Minecraft materials
applied to the same historical shapes. **Diamond and netherite are gameplay adaptations, not
historical claims.**

| Tier | Blade | Rapier hilt | Zweihänder hilt / grip | Katana fittings / ito |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wooden | wood (training weapon) | dark wood, leather grip | dark wood, tan leather | bokken: all wood |
| Stone | knapped stone | dark stone, leather grip | dark stone, brown leather | dark stone, brown ito |
| Copper | copper with verdigris flecks | bronze, copper wire | bronze, brown leather | copper, green ito |
| Iron | **bright steel (historical)** | **blued steel, silver wire** | **blackened iron, black leather** | **shakudō, iron tsuba, kon ito** |
| Golden | gold | gilt, gold wire | gilt, red velvet-like grip | gold fittings, black ito |
| Diamond | diamond (adaptation) | silver | steel, black leather | shibuichi, white ito |
| Netherite | netherite (adaptation) | blackened steel with gold inlay | netherite, red leather | shakudō with gold, purple ito |
